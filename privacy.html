Privacy policy

Last updated 2 November 2026.

SOUP Tools ("SOUP") is a desktop app that runs on your Windows PC. It has no servers and no accounts of its own, and it does not collect analytics. Your replays, stats and settings stay on your PC unless you choose to back them up or share them.

What SOUP connects to

  • Riot's client and services, to read your own match history and replays. Read-only, your own account.
  • GitHub, to check for SOUP updates and to download extensions you choose to install.
  • valorant-api.com, for public game data such as agent and map names.
  • Google, only when you use the Drive Sync extension (below).

Google user data (Drive Sync)

When you sign in with Google in the Drive Sync extension, SOUP asks for:

  • Your email address (email, openid), to show which Google account is signed in.
  • Access to files SOUP creates or that you open with it (drive.file). SOUP cannot see any other file in your Google Drive.

How it is used: to upload your SOUP backups (replay library, match files and SOUP settings) to a "SOUP Sync" folder in your Drive, to download them again when you restore, and to keep a team folder you share in sync. Nothing else.

Where it goes: directly between your PC and Google. The developer of SOUP never receives your Google data, your files or your sign-in tokens. They are not sold, not shared with anyone, not used for advertising and not used to train AI models.

Storage: the sign-in token is kept in Windows Credential Manager on your PC. Your files are stored in your own Google Drive, under your Google account's terms.

Sharing: a team folder is shared only with the people you invite, using Google Drive's own sharing.

Deleting: Sign out in Drive Sync's settings, or remove access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Uninstalling the extension deletes the token from your PC. The "SOUP Sync" folder stays in your Drive until you delete it there.

SOUP Tools' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Bug reports

"Report a Bug" opens a GitHub issue in your browser and a diagnostics file on your PC. Nothing is sent unless you submit the issue yourself, and you can read the file first.
